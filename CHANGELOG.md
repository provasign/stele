# Changelog

## v0.1.15 - 2026-11-10

Found by a real Claude Code session adopting a held-out project of 179 requirements from one sentence of its owner's. That session read everything at once, had its first command killed at Claude Code's ten-minute limit, answered 19 questions of Stele's itself over three more rounds, and after 42 minutes and $3.45 brought its owner 22 contradictions, 17 of them with nothing recommended. A review of the specs' texts backed one of the 22.

- **At an adoption the owner is asked only about contradictions a reading of the texts also finds.** The review of the texts says what deserves the owner, the solver proves it, and what only Stele's rules show Stele settles itself, in the same run: by precedence where one requirement is a check or an error case, else recorded as open. The summary and the approval say how many, and the report lists each. On two labelled sets every contradiction the rules proved was among the review's findings; on the held-out project the rule clashes outside them were gaps no reader sees ("SHALL create" against a check before it), steps taken to run on an existing instance, and misreadings. Without a review (the model call failed, or the limit on spending was reached) every clash is asked, as before.
- **No rounds for the agent at an adoption.** "Does this step create it", "which step makes a new one" and a reading that contradicts itself were questions for the agent, each round a rerun of several minutes with the owner waiting. Stele answers them itself and checks again within the run.
- **A large project is offered a part first.** A bare `stele adopt` on more than a hundred requirements in several specs reads nothing and spends nothing: it answers with status `choose`, each spec with its size and about what reading it takes, and the agent asks the owner which to start with. `stele adopt --all` reads everything.
- **A check stops itself before the agent's command limit.** Run with `--json`, a check works for at most eight minutes, keeps its work, and answers with status `continue` and how far it is; the agent runs the same command again at once. The first reading of 86 requirements ran past ten minutes, was killed, and the agent waited nine more for word from a dead process. `--timeout` sets another limit, and `STELE_SLICE` another slice.
- **Asked again with nothing changed, a check answers at once** with the result it kept, and what it prints is half the size: the glossary under every requirement of every question, and the full lists of new terms, are left to the report. An agent whose tool cut the JSON short had run the whole check three more times to read it.
- **The same session, run again from the owner's same sentence:** a choice of specs after five seconds and six cents, where the first session read for 42 minutes; then, for two specs of 86 requirements, one to three contradictions for the owner in three runs, each one a reader of the specs would also find (two specs naming one status differently, which of two jobs closes an incident), against 22. That reading still took 16 to 19 minutes: these sessions ran Stele's calls on the account's default model, a larger and slower one than the Sonnet every other figure here was measured on.
- **A part of the specs is reviewed with what is approved,** so that a contradiction between a spec being adopted and one adopted earlier has its backing.

## v0.1.14 - 2026-11-10

- **Finding a contradiction at an adoption no longer hangs on one reading.** Whether the rules showed a contradiction depended on how each requirement happened to be read: two adoptions of one labelled set of 45 requirements found 8 and 5 of its 13 known contradictions. An adoption now also has the model review the requirements' texts twice for contradictions, for cents. A contradiction the texts suggest and the rules do not show has its requirements read once more, told what the review saw, and a contradiction the rules then show is asked as before. One they still do not show is listed for the owner as possible, in one sentence with the requirements it involves and why Stele could not prove it, before the approval. It needs no answer; the owner may reword a requirement or approve knowing of it. Possible contradictions that share a requirement are one item. On the 45-requirement set, two adoptions now showed 12 and 13 of the 13 (8 and 7 proved by the rules); on a labelled set of 109 requirements, 19 of 20 (16 proved). An adoption costs about $0.40 more at that size. The list also names contradictions the labels do not, mostly real ones ("deleting an account deletes its invoices, which must never be deleted"). On the 179-requirement held-out project, the readings of one adoption did not show the contradiction that adoption was for, two specs naming one status differently; it was listed as possible, one of two items, both real. That first check cost $4.04 and took about 13 minutes, against $3.96 and 10.

## v0.1.13 - 2026-11-10

- **A project's specs can be adopted a few at a time.** `stele adopt --list` prints each spec with how many requirements it holds and whether it is read, without calling a model. `stele adopt --spec <name>[,<name>]` reads only the specs named; the summary names those not read yet, and a later `stele adopt --spec` reads more of them, checked against what is approved. Each part keeps its rules and its open conflicts in its own registry files, and the registry records which specs were read (`adopted-specs.txt`). A contradiction with a spec adopted earlier can be decided later, or settled by the spec being read taking precedence, like one within a part. On a held-out project, a spec of 46 requirements was approved with no question for its owner ($1.18), and a second of 30 was then read against it and approved.
- **A step that sets a value keeps what a rule ties to it, also when both are new.** "Lending prints a receipt" against "a receipt lists the due date" came to the owner as a contradiction: the step was taken to set the receipt and leave what it lists untouched. Stele already let a step write a new value that a state rule ties to one it writes, but only when the value it writes was approved earlier, and at an adoption nothing is. On the second spec above, the owner was asked 10 questions over four rounds, the same ones returning after each answer; with this, 3 in one round, on the same readings.
- **A question the owner answered stays the owner's.** When one requirement already prevailed and the two still clashed, the question went to the agent for a reread, the agent's answer replaced the owner's, and the owner was asked a third time.
- **The preview guide says how to adopt existing specs:** what to expect in time, cost and questions, and how to start with one or two specs.

## v0.1.12 - 2026-11-10

Found by adopting a held-out project of 179 requirements, and a 76-requirement part of it, end to end, as a new user would.

- **An adoption names each fact once.** When the requirements to translate span more than one model request (an adoption, a large change), Stele first proposes the facts over all of them, and every request reuses those names. Requests sent at once used to name one fact each their own way, and rules over different names never meet. On two held-out sets with independent labels, the check then found 16 of 20 and 8 of 13 known contradictions, against 4 and 4, and 25 of its 26 findings across requirements held a labelled one; the spend rose from $1.21 to $1.70 and from $0.56 to $0.66. A change of 20 requirements or fewer is translated as before, and a check already under way keeps the way it started.
- **`stele adopt` runs again on a project with OpenSpec, Spec Kit or Kiro specs.** v0.1.11 refused it with "this project's specs are not adopted yet" and sent the owner back to `stele adopt`: the check that sends a change before adoption to adopt took the adoption itself for such a change. Projects with a `requirements/` folder or Shale requests were not affected.
- **A first adoption no longer ends with "nothing is checked yet".** When the model gives no rules for a few requirements, the check reads them once more itself and reports what stays so as unchecked, instead of stopping as failed and asking for the same command again.
- **A clash the code shows to be none goes to the agent first.** When the code does what both requirements say in the situation Stele found, the agent has Stele read them again with what the code does as the note, and the owner is asked only if they still clash. Four of the twelve conflicts a first adoption asked its owner were of this kind, each with nothing recommended.
- **Requirements that share a number name their spec.** Two specs with a "Requirement 5 (R5.1)" appear in a question as "loans: Requirement 5 (R5.1)" and "returns: Requirement 5 (R5.1)".
- **The summary counts unchecked requirements when there are many** (it named 59, "Requirement 4" over and over); the report lists each.
- **`--max-cost` covers the whole check.** The calls that read the code for each conflict came on top of it: an adoption capped at $3 spent $3.66. Past the limit, conflicts are asked without what the code does.
- **A question is asked once.** Two pairs of new names for one fact gave the same question twice.
- **At an adoption, a contradiction can be decided later.** Every conflict question of `stele adopt` now offers "Decide later": the contradiction is recorded in the registry as an open conflict the owner acknowledged, the approval says how many stay open, `stele check` lists them, and the gate lets no new one join them. The registry and the gate have held open conflicts all along; the questions never offered them, so a first adoption could not be approved until every contradiction in years of specs was settled. On the 179-requirement project the owner faced 12, then 8, then 13 conflicts over three rounds; with this, the adoption was approved with 15 left open.
- **A requirement Stele stopped at for a missing fact is read once more.** A reply often gave up on a requirement because the vocabulary had no name for something it could have declared itself ("the member has no late-return count"), and the requirement was recorded unchecked. Such a requirement is now read once more, told to declare the attribute or step and to write a colour code or a label with spaces as a string. On a held-out set of 109 requirements with independent labels, 2 stayed without vocabulary against 24, 86 were read in full against 58, and the check found 16 of 20 known contradictions as before, for $1.98 against $1.70. On the 179-requirement project none stayed without vocabulary, against 28, and 40 requirements were left unchecked after the first check, against 59. The facts recovered brought findings the first adoption could not see: values two specs give that the code does not use.
- **A clashing rule that does not say what its text says is set aside, not read again.** Before the owner is asked about a conflict, each of its rules is checked against its own requirement's text, in one model call for all conflicts. A rule with its condition turned round, or one that makes a step run where the text only says what the step does, is set aside: the requirement keeps its other rules, the summary says how many were set aside, and the report names each. Reading a requirement again to settle a clash gave a new reading that clashed elsewhere, each round moving one or two clashes to other pairs; a rule set aside can only make the conflicts fewer. On the 179-requirement project 6 rules were set aside over three rounds, each wrong by a reading by hand, and none of a real contradiction.
- **The first adoption of the 179-requirement project, with all of the above:** the first check asked its owner 5 conflicts, each with a recommended answer, against 12 with none; it cost $3.96 against $3.66 and took about ten minutes. The adoption was approved after three rounds of answers with 135 requirements checked against 120, 44 unchecked against 59, 2 conflicts settled by precedence and 11 decided later, for $5.41 in all, and `stele check` passes on it. One run each: a second run of a 45-requirement labelled set, on a path none of this changes, found 5 of 13 known contradictions where the first found 8, so differences of this size between single runs are not evidence.
- **A requirement recorded as one yes/no fact is not counted as checked.** A requirement about how the code is built ("use the shared client") is sometimes read as a single yes/no fact that no other requirement reads. Its rule can clash with nothing yet, so the summary and the approval now count such requirements apart from the checked ones and say what Stele would catch for them: a later requirement that says the opposite. They were counted as checked, and the count moved from run to run with how many were read so.
- **Found by an agent adopting the 76-requirement project from the project's own instructions alone:**
  - An agent's note is no longer given to the translation as the owner's word. An agent told to say what the code does had its note read as "the owner says", and a requirement to show an expiry message came out forbidding the step to succeed. The note now reaches the translation as a reviewer's, to be followed only where the requirement's own words say so, and the agent is told to say what the text means, not what the code does.
  - `stele change --json` and `stele adopt --json` save what they print as `summary.json` in the change's folder: an adoption's JSON is tens of kilobytes, more than some agents' tools show of one command's output. The skill says so, and that exit code 1 with JSON printed is no failure.
  - The skill describes the `creator` and `free_setting` questions, and says to answer one's own questions and check again before the owner is asked, so the owner gets theirs in one round.
  - The summary counts a clash of several requirements as a contradiction (it said "2 other decisions" where the next step counted four contradictions), and says "1 decision is needed" when there is no clash for "other" to refer to.
  - The question about a value no requirement fixes asks whether it is one fixed value or one that is configured or comes from the data, and recommends neither.
  - A merged conflict gives one reason for its recommendation, not one sentence for each pair. Where the code keeps the two requirements apart, the reason says the answer records that order.
  - A clash that comes back after the agent's reread, where the code does what both requirements say, recommends "Decide later".
  - A step's history reads as words in an example ("validate has run", not "ran(validate)").
  - During an adoption, a reread points to `stele adopt` (it said `stele change`), and the approval speaks of "these specs" and "the adoption", not "this change".
- **Where the code already makes the exception, precedence is recommended that way.** The recommended answer was "say so in the spec", which asks an adopting owner to edit it.
- **A contradiction decided later may rest on a step's run history.** Recording one failed the whole adoption with "pin rules as ID@version".
- **At an adoption, one requirement can be the exception to another, with no edit to the spec.** A plain "SHALL record the loan" against "verify that the member exists before lending", or "print a receipt" against "when the database is down, show an error page", is not a contradiction to any reader: the check comes first. Such a conflict now offers "X is an exception to Y" both ways, and recommends the error case, or else the check, as the exception. The answer is recorded as the rules' precedence (D-052), with no new reading. On the 179-requirement project, 6 of the 15 conflicts came with this recommendation, each right by a reading by hand, and taking them left 10 open instead of 15; on the 76-requirement part, both conflicts were settled this way and none stays open. A requirement applying in narrower situations alone is no longer recommended as an exception at an adoption: "set the status to RETURNED_LATE" against "set it to RETURNED" can be two specs naming one status differently. When one requirement already prevails and the two still clash, the question says Stele has probably misread one of them and recommends deciding later, instead of offering the same answer again.
- **A check before a step prevails where it fails.** Precedence of a precondition over another rule weakened that rule where the precondition's condition held, which is where it does not bite; it now weakens it where the condition fails.
- **"Decide later" covers the same clash as a step that can never run.** With the conflicts decided later known, the same requirements could show as a step that never runs, which no question covered: the adoption ended as "broken" with nothing to act on. The summary no longer says the requirements agree when some contradictions were decided later.
- **`stele check` passes a registry the gate accepted.** A rule fixing a value ("results are sorted by due date") makes the value's others impossible, as it says; the gate set these aside and `stele check` failed on them, so an adopted registry failed its first check. The check now lists them as fixed by their rule.
- **A reading that contradicts itself twice can be left unchecked.** The owner was asked what Stele had misread, or to reword the requirement. The first, recommended answer now keeps the requirement and records it without rules.
- **"Does this step create it?" comes with an answer** when the step's name or its requirements' words say so (create, add, register, "a new ..."). Answered wrongly, three clashes no new instance can have went to the owner.
- **A crash is fixed:** checking two contradicting requirements about the state that name no subject ("an order MUST be paid", "an order MUST NOT be paid") stopped `stele change` with a nil pointer.
- **The approval question counts unchecked requirements when there are many,** as the summary does.

## v0.1.11 - 2026-11-09

Found by live trials with Claude Code on an invented lending project, in headless runs and in a recorded terminal session.

- **A project with no spec tool gets a `requirements/` folder.** `stele init` sets up plain Markdown, one list item for each requirement with an ID and a MUST, MUST NOT or SHOULD. The agent writes them, and `stele change markdown .` checks whatever the folder holds that the owner has not approved. A new repository needs no first commit.
- **Approval is one act.** The agent runs the command that approves and Claude Code asks the owner to allow it, with the check's summary in the prompt, even where an allow rule covers Stele's commands. Where no one can be asked (permissions bypassed, or a headless run that holds the prompt), the agent asks in chat and adds `--asked` on a yes. `STELE_APPROVAL_PROMPT=off` is for unattended runs. The prompt names the change only where the project names its changes, and carries nothing meant for the agent.
- **A request that cannot keep an approved requirement stops the agent first.** The note Stele hands the agent with a request now says to stop before any edit, quote the requirement and ask the owner what should hold. In one recorded session the agent had rewritten the requirement before asking.
- **A project's first requirement keeps its rules.** Pruning dropped attributes a rule read only through the variable of `instances(E)`, so the check called the change clean with nothing to approve. Pruning can no longer cost a rule: when a rule compiles with the unpruned vocabulary and not the pruned one, the unpruned one is kept.
- **A requirement none of whose readings compile** fails once, is read again on the next check, and read so twice goes to the approval as a requirement Stele records and does not check.
- **Findings are split.** What the owner decides on (a reading broader than the text, or one that blocks a step) is listed; clauses no rule checks are a count, with the list in the report. A clean approval is one line. The summary no longer says a requirement with no rule agrees with the approved ones, and names a Markdown requirement by its own line.
- **Quieter instructions.** `stele init` writes only the instructions the project's format needs, and every message names one next step. The agent leaves lessons out of its report when an approval adds none. `stele --help` lists the commands a project uses; `stele help all` the rest.
- **README and docs.** The README opens with a recording of a real Claude Code session, and compares Stele with Kiro, Spec Kit and OpenSpec from their public documentation.

- **Documentation brought up to date.** The docs describe Claude Code as the one supported agent, the flow without a spec tool (Shale requests, `stele keep`, `stele export openspec`) as workflow W6, the repository layout `stele init` writes, and the reachability check as built. The translation document now describes the pipeline as built and lists the original designs that are not (extractors, auto-accept, code anchors, synonym index, domain packs) in one section. Decision tables, `final` rules and checks of `measured` rules are marked not built, where some pages had described them as working. References to the private research repository and to a private design document were removed. The README links the known limits and where to report problems.

## v0.1.10 - 2026-11-09

- **Stele supports Claude Code only, for now.** `stele init` detects, offers and sets up Claude Code alone, and refuses another agent by name ("not supported in this preview"); it picks the claude CLI or an Anthropic API key to translate with. The README, the preview guide and the agent instructions name only Claude Code. A project whose `stele.yaml` already records Codex or another agent keeps it: init still refreshes their files, and `translate.llm: codex` or `openai` still runs.
- **The agent hook finds specs written in YAML's brace form** (`{"specs": ...}`): it looked only for `specs:` at the start of a line and stayed silent in such a project. Projects set up by `stele init` were not affected.

## v0.1.9 - 2026-11-09

Found by a live trial of both flows with Claude Code on an invented project: an OpenSpec feature from adoption to build, and four requests on a project without a spec tool (about $3.80 in all).

- **A role a requirement names is a value of the actor's role.** The platform's actor role and kind start empty for a project to extend, and a reading that compared the actor with "librarian" failed on a fresh registry unless the model also declared the role, which nothing told it to do. Names a rule compares them with are now their values. An adoption of two requirements failed on its first run this way.
- **A failed check no longer blames the model command.** A reading Stele could not compile is reported in the same place as a model call that failed; the next step now names both, and says the spec needs no rewording.
- **The summary of a check with nothing approved yet** says the requirements agree with each other, not "with the approved requirements".
- **The approval question no longer counts new terms.** Agents relayed them to the owner as Stele's internal names. The report still lists them, marked as Stele's names for the agent.
- **The report says how to read its rules.** An agent read `count(p in instances(Packet): ...) <= 2` as a limit of two, told the owner a correct reading of "at most three" was off by one, and spent two turns and a reread making it weaker. `instances(E)` is every other instance, never `self`; the report and the skill now say so, and that a rule that only looks wrong is no reason to reread.
- **A "cannot happen" lesson keeps the owner's words** without the solver's situation the answer carries for the reread.
- **A clash that needs another instance in a state no step leaves is set aside.** Stele already set aside a clash whose step acts on an instance no step could have left so; it now tests every other instance the situation has as well. Two readings of "holds" (borrowed and held by the member; held by the member) clashed only when another packet was held yet available, which no step leaves, and the owner was asked whether that could happen. A step that can leave such a packet keeps the clash.
- **A clash's example names each instance once,** with its values, and leaves out slots that hold no instance: "packet: other #1: holder id is <other text 1>, status is borrowed", not one entry per value with "no packet: other #4" beside that packet's values. The step's own instance comes first.
- **Agent instructions:** `--quoted` is for the owner's words copied as said, never a paraphrase or a prefix such as "Owner:" (the trial's agent quoted its own summary of "Exception."); lessons are described without their IDs; a request is kept before building only when the owner says it lasts, and a request worded as a job is built first, its rule offered once after.

## v0.1.8 - 2026-11-08

- **A check reads its prompt from the cache in every wave.** The vocabulary a check starts from goes in the cached system prompt, and the terms its own earlier waves added go in each request, so repairs and rereads read the same cached prompt instead of writing a new one. Before, every new term changed the prompt: a two-requirement check on v0.1.6 sent a 15,000-token prompt five times and read none of it from the cache. On a two-requirement check with Sonnet it cost $0.12 instead of $0.17, in 40 seconds instead of 48.
- **Translation replies group rules under their requirement,** which names its overrides once. Replies are about 3% shorter per rule. With the cache change, three runs of the 60-requirement held-out set cost $1.12 to $1.21 (before: $1.34 to $1.50), with 12 to 19 clauses unchecked (before: 9 to 24); one of the three left 17 requirements needing vocabulary where the others left 0 and 5.
- **Rereads run in smaller parallel calls.** The request-size limit now counts the reread note, which is longer than its requirement, so the rereads of a 60-requirement set went in four calls of up to 35 seconds instead of two of 72 to 79; the whole read took 3:01 instead of 3:49 to 4:56, for $1.10 with the readings in the earlier range.
- **The synonym check asks about at least 30 pairs per call** instead of 10: a two-requirement check against a 137-attribute vocabulary asked about 25 pairs in three calls, $0.22 of its $1.31 on v0.1.6, to find nothing.
- **The question about a request with nothing to check says what it asks.** It now says the request states no behaviour the product keeps, and spells out both answers: a one-off job, done when the work is done and never approved as a requirement, or a requirement the product keeps from now on, recorded without being checked. The agent instructions tell the agent to put it with both answers. Before, "found nothing to check ... a requirement or a one-off task?" read as a judgement on the request, and an owner answered "task" for a requirement they meant to keep.
- **A request declared with `shale intent` is a task unless it is kept.** Only requests kept as requirements (`stele keep`, recorded in `.stele/kept-intents.yaml`) are a Shale project's specs; the agent keeps one when the owner asks for something the product must keep from now on, and `stele keep --drop` makes it a task again. Before, every request was a requirement: chores, trials and reviews became questions for the owner, and the agent's working notes were read as rules (29 requests and ten questions waited at once on this repository). Requests a project approved before are kept the first time Stele reads it.
- **Only the owner's own words teach Stele.** A note becomes a lesson only when it is marked `--quoted`, the owner's words as they said them. A note the agent wrote, also one explaining the owner's one-word answer, guides the check and teaches nothing.
- **The agent rereads a requirement once per change.** Stele refuses a second reread note or `misread` answer from the agent for the same requirement; the reading then stands, and the owner sees what it misses among the findings at the approval. Notes had chased each other, each reread fixing one reading and making another too wide.
- **Several questions for the owner are announced.** When more than one waits, the next step tells the agent to say how many and what about before the first.
- **The agent hook says nothing in a project with no specs configured,** and says a failure to check once, not after every command.
- **The check's JSON carries a `summary`:** the check in the owner's words (how many requirements, checked against how many, whether they agree, what Stele cannot check, what needs deciding), for the agent to show under the spec it drafted, so the owner approves the spec and Stele's reading in one act.
- **`stele keep --decline` and `--list`.** The owner's no to keeping a request sets it aside for good, so no agent offers it again; the list shows each request's state. The agent instructions say to offer a lasting rule once, when the work is done, and never to ask about chores.
- **`stele export openspec`** writes a Shale project's kept requests as its first OpenSpec spec; the approved rules follow their statements under the new IDs, `stele.yaml` names OpenSpec, and Shale stays as the record of tasks.
- **The agent instructions run the check while the draft is finished,** before the spec is shown, and name the spec change a task implements with `shale intent --for`.
- Tried and not kept, measured on the same set: Sonnet at medium effort cost no less ($1.50); batches of 10 requirements with 8 calls in parallel cost more ($1.43), left 11 requirements needing vocabulary and invented two names for one fact; a reread that names the rules it keeps instead of writing them out saved a quarter of the reread but recovered 7 of 32 requirements where the full rewrite recovers 16 to 25.

## v0.1.7 - 2026-11-06

- **Stele's calls run on the coding agent you are using, and its model.** `translate.llm: agent`, which `stele init` now writes when Claude Code or Codex is installed, resolves at each run to the agent Stele is run from: Claude Code by its environment, Codex and Claude Code by a record the hook keeps under `.stele/cache/` on every prompt and model switch. The model is the one that agent is using at the time (Codex names it in every hook event; Claude Code at session start and on `/model`), else the CLI's own default. Outside any agent, in CI or a shell, `agent` runs on the installed CLI, else an API key's provider. `claude`, `codex`, `anthropic`, `openai` and `cmd` still name one outright, and `translate.model` still pins a model: on a 60-requirement held-out set `claude-sonnet-5-5` read the whole set, rereads included, for $1.34 in 5 minutes where Opus cost $5.03 and 21 minutes of model time, and the same judge scored Sonnet's rules at 111 clauses covered to 55 uncovered against Opus's 122 to 26. Reasoning effort stays Stele's own (the agent's low effort would cut what the rules cover).
- **Stele works under Codex's sandbox.** Stele calls the model through your codex CLI, which cannot start inside the sandbox Codex runs commands in ("failed to initialize in-process app-server client: Operation not permitted"), so every check run from Codex failed. `stele init` now writes `.codex/rules/stele.rules`, which lets `stele change` and `stele adopt` run outside the sandbox once Codex trusts the project, and a check that still runs inside it stops at once and says to run it outside the sandbox, instead of retrying every statement.
- **Stele knows it runs inside Codex without the project's hooks.** Codex marks the commands it runs with its session, so `agent` resolves to Codex even before `/hooks` is reviewed; the model the hook recorded applies only to the session that recorded it.
- **Agents wait for the check.** Codex ran `stele change` in the background, wrote the feature while it ran and stopped it after a stall; the instructions now say a check takes a few minutes, to wait for its JSON, and to edit only after the owner's approval.
- **A reread that runs to the request limit is not sent again;** the statement keeps its earlier reading. On Codex the same reread stalled twice for 280 seconds each.
- **A rule that takes the length of a map is refused with what to write instead** (which keys it holds with `has`, what it holds with `m[k]`). Told only what `len` takes, the model's repairs of a listing written as a map ran to the request limit, in each of two runs on Codex.
- **An answer about a step a later reading no longer has is left out,** instead of stopping the check: a reread named the entity differently, and "the step creates the plot" left the change's registry unloadable (`extend "Plot": no such entity`).
- **`stele init` run again records a spec format set up after it,** such as Shale's requests after `shale init`; Stele did not read them.
- **The translation prompt is 14,000 characters instead of 23,900.** A held-out comparison read as well as before (101 clauses covered to 50 uncovered, against 95 to 49). Low reasoning effort was tried and dropped: it covered 53.
- **The calls of a translation wave read their shared prompt from the cache.** The shared vocabulary and the code's structure are in the system prompt, and before a wave of several calls a small call that asks for nothing writes it to the cache, so the wave's calls read it instead of each writing it. Only an identical system prompt is read from the cache: the same text at the start of the request is written again by every call. On a 60-requirement set with no approved vocabulary the saving is within run-to-run variation, since the model's output is three quarters of a translation's cost; it grows with a project's approved vocabulary.
- **The synonym check describes each attribute once per call,** instead of again in every pair it is in: its prompts are about half as long, and it cost a third less per pair on the 60-requirement set.
- **The agent's reminder about requests waiting for the owner names this session's requests and counts the others,** instead of listing every waiting request each time one is added, and says the command to run once.
- **A relation to many that the model also marks optional loads,** instead of costing a repair call: a relation to many may be empty already.

## v0.1.6 - 2026-11-06

- **A step's rule is about the instance the step names, unless it says otherwise.** When a step's rules tie the instance to a request field of the same name (`request.number = self.number`), a rule on that step that reads the instance and nothing of that request field is read for the named instance only. "A released plot with a holder goes to the first member waiting" was read for every held plot, and clashed with "releasing an unknown number is rejected"; five rereads and a rewording did not settle it.
- **An exception names the requirement in the spec its text names.** "An exception to FR-006 of the waitlist spec (005-waitlist)" overrode the new spec's own FR-006, and the clash the owner had settled as an exception came back.
- **A world fact added after a rejected one gets its own ID.** It took the ID of the last one, and the change's registry no longer loaded ("rule world-main-3 declared twice").
- **A state rule over another entity's instances holds for the other instances a step looks at.** "A member on the waitlist holds no plot" held for the member a step acted on, not for the first member waiting when a plot was released, and the owner was asked whether that member could already hold a plot.
- **Approved steps can be corrected.** A registry approved before Stele learned to tell a value a step reports from one it writes kept those writes, and nothing corrected them: a listing could change any tool, so clashes no step reaches stood and steps went undecided. When a change check meets such a step (the change's rules are on it, or a clash or an undecided check is in it), it asks the agent whether the step changes each value it only shows in its result. The answer "keeps" is recorded in the change's registry (`extend: Op` with `keeps: [...]`), and the approval names it. Found in all three trial registries (every listing), and once in 160 benchmark registries.
- **`stele init` on a Codex-only machine** sets Codex as the translation model: it chose the claude CLI whatever was installed, and every translation failed. It picks the claude CLI, else the codex CLI, else an API key's provider, and says what to install when there is none.
- **`stele init` on a new Spec Kit, OpenSpec or Kiro project** keeps the spec layout in `stele.yaml` when there is no spec yet, instead of reporting it as unreadable and leaving it out.
- **Checks on Codex run at medium reasoning effort** unless `translate.effort` in `stele.yaml` or `--effort` says otherwise. They ran at the user's own Codex default: at high, a six-requirement Spec Kit change took 7 to 20 minutes, calls stalling past the request limit and retried whole; at medium, 3 to 5 minutes with no stall, and readings as good.
- **`stele init` says when Node.js is missing.** The Claude Code and Codex hooks run with `node`; without it they failed and nothing said why.
- **A requirement the model reads with no rules twice is unchecked, not failed.** It held its change back on every check, and the agent offered to reword the spec to suit Stele; now the second reading of the same text goes on to the approval, each clause no rule checks a finding. The first time, `next` says to run the check again rather than to fix the model command.
- **A Codex call that stalls is sent again after 100 seconds,** and the first answer is used; each attempt has the whole request limit. A few calls stall until the 3-minute request limit, and the same request sent again answers in under a minute.
- **A step a change proposed on an earlier check writes what later rules set after it.** The step was read as keeping the value, and the change reported clashes that were not there.
- **A new attribute of an entity is not a change to the step that creates it.** A change that gave plots a holder made adding a plot "write" the holder, and the owner was asked, twice, whether adding a plot changed meaning; no approved rule reads a new attribute.
- **Rules a change adds to an existing step read reports as reports.** "Listing the tools also shows each lent tool's due date" made the listing write the tool's id and lent date: the fix in v0.1.5 covered new steps only.

## v0.1.5 - 2026-11-05

Found by three developer trials (ten features each, built with Claude Code and Stele from the owner's requests) and a code review.

- **The owner's decisions take fewer turns.**
  - **Precedence:** a new request that adds a case to an approved one can take precedence where they meet, both standing; no restating of the whole rule.
  - **Restated requests:** a request restated after the owner retired the earlier wording is the same requirement, edited, with no rename question.
  - **World facts:** precedence over a request no longer weakens the facts about the world it states (a command's text), which had turned them into clashes with unrelated steps.
  - **Denied precedence:** "MUST NOT override FR-001" no longer reads as overriding FR-001.
  - **Changed numbers:** a request whose rule is an approved rule with only its numbers changed ("at most 2" for "at most 3") asks whether it replaces it, however it is worded.
- **More of what a request says is checked, and the rest is named.**
  - **Exact computed values decide:** "the whole days overdue", stated by two bounds, and "the message names the overdue tool" are decided where the solver used to give up.
  - **Results per item decide:** a uniqueness rule makes its attribute a key, so the tools a step reads with the same id are one tool, and results keyed by id have one value each.
  - **Other instances:** state invariants hold for the other instances a rule ranges over.
  - **Partial checks are named:** a reading Stele could not use is a finding of its requirement, so a requirement checked in part says which part is not.
  - **Free settings are asked about:** a threshold read through a new setting no requirement gives a value is a question: the owner says whether a fixed value was meant.
  - **Undecided steps:** the owner can approve a step the solver cannot decide; the registry records it as unchecked and CI's gate accepts only recorded steps.
- **A missed contradiction is found.** "Stewards may have up to 5 tools lent" beside a 3-tool limit read as an error that changes nothing passed, since the refused lend still ran. Being able to do something now means the step runs and changes something it writes.
- **Fewer false contradictions.**
  - **Unreachable states:** a clash in a state no step reaches is set aside more often. Reachability now fixes whether optional values are set and leaves out entities with no creating step. When recording which step creates an entity would set a clash aside, the agent is asked which one. A clash is set aside only when its rules clash in no reachable state, not when the one state the solver sampled is unreachable.
  - **Lists:** lists fit the instances they hold.
  - **Kept optionals:** `x = old(x)` keeps an optional value that is unset.
  - **Write inference:** reads of other instances, and values a step only reports (a key it looks a result up by, a value compared with its result), are no longer taken as writes.
  - **Pruning:** keeps what a rule that does not compile yet names.
- **Clearer questions.**
  - **Examples:** a question's example keeps each slot's values ("shed: tools #2: id is 0").
  - **Statuses:** an undecided check reads as `undecided`, not `broken`.
  - **Agent reminders:** the hook reminds the agent of requests the owner has not approved, and asks it to check and report the approved requirements a change touches.
- **Commits and approval.**
  - **Base branch:** in a Shale project, a commit to the base branch waits for the owner's approval of the requests it carries (on a branch, the merge waits).
  - **Staged content:** the pre-commit hook checks the staged specs, not the working tree.
  - **Exact approval:** approval applies exactly the registry the check produced.
  - **Gate:** a head that removes an entity another step wrote no longer panics the gate.
  - **Removals:** a change that only removes requirements comes to the approval, which retires their rules; it used to report clean with the rules still live.
- **Caching.** A cached step result re-decides reachability on every check, since another step's change can make its clashing state reachable. A check that was cancelled or ran out of time is never cached.
- **Processes and timeouts.** Solvers share one set of warm z3 processes per binary, so a long-running `stele serve` no longer gains z3 processes with every check. The glossary and code-evidence model calls of `stele change` have the per-request timeout, like translation.
- **Coverage failures are told.** A requirement the coverage check could not judge is a "not judged" finding in the approval, and is judged again on the next check.
- **Translation.** The coverage judge reads a listing of the items that meet a condition as exactly those. Translation reads a refused step as changing nothing, a record as kept on the step's own instance, and an output of an optional value as optional.
- **Grove v0.63.27.** The index folder ignores itself, so `git add -A` no longer commits it; the release also memoizes the Python re-export walk (v0.63.26).
- Watch tests no longer flake on a half-written file.

## v0.1.4 - 2026-11-03

- **A skipped translation is not a one-off task.** A model can answer with placeholders instead of rules ("The requested rule translation is not provided in this response"). The coverage judge now also reads a statement whose readings are all informal: when it finds a clause a rule can check, the statement is translated again, and if it still has no rule it is reported under `failed`, to be retried, instead of being asked about as a one-off task.
- **Shale's evidence rides the code commit.** The agent instructions say to run `shale done` before committing, so the commit carries the request's evidence with the code (Shale 0.2.3 stages it). A Shale session that ends for good closes its request as `shale done` does, and a request still open when a commit recorded the closed ones is read as its own.
- `stele init` adds its check to an existing pre-commit hook (Shale's, a linter's) instead of skipping it, and never rewrites a hook that already runs Stele.

## v0.1.3 - 2026-11-03

- **Each task of a session is its own request.** An agent working through several features in one conversation recorded them in one Shale session, and only the latest request counted: the next feature read as an edit of the approved one before. A completion (`shale done`) now closes a request, and the next one is `intent/<session>-c<n>`, the name Shale 0.2.2 gives its file; a request declared again before the completion still restates it.
- **A one-off task is not a requirement.** With Shale requests as the specs, a request Stele finds nothing to check in ("write the review", "set up the tracker") comes back as a question for the owner: a requirement the product must keep, or a one-off task. A task is set aside in `.stele/retired-intents.yaml`, marked "not a requirement", and leaves the change instead of being approved as a requirement that protects nothing.

## v0.1.2 - 2026-11-03

Found by the developer trial in Codex, continued.

- **A model call that stalls no longer stalls the check.** `stele change` and `stele adopt` limit each model call (`--request-timeout`, 3 minutes by default, as `stele formalize` does); a call that stalls fails and is retried, and a requirement whose calls keep failing is reported under `failed`. A run that still hits its overall `--timeout` prints its JSON with status `failed` and the error, instead of none.
- **Stopping Stele stops the model CLI it runs.** The CLI runs in its own process group, which a SIGTERM or ^C to Stele did not reach; it was left running. Stele now kills it first.
- **Regular expressions in rules compile.** A string such as `"\s*"`, written with one backslash, failed with "bad string"; an escape Go does not know now keeps its backslash, as the regular expression means.
- **The coverage judge knows what `instances(E)` means:** every other instance, never `self`. It had flagged the uniqueness rule `all(t in instances(Task): t.id != self.id)` as forbidding every task.
- A translation run an earlier release finished with failures recorded is started again, so the first check after upgrading retries them.

## v0.1.1 - 2026-11-03

Found by using Stele with Shale on its own repository, and by a developer trial in Codex.

- **`stele check` in a project,** with no registry named, checks every approved requirement on the branch against every other and reports each contradiction as the requirements it involves, their sources and who approved them. Run it after a merge or pull: changes approved on different branches can contradict each other once they meet.
- **A translation that fails is a failure, not a clean check.** When the model backend fails (not logged in, blocked by a sandbox, no network) or gives no valid reading after its repairs, `stele change` and `stele adopt` report status `failed`, list the requirements under `failed` with the error, exit 1, and offer no approval. Before, the check said `clean` and exited 0, and the failure was cached so that a retry never called the model again; a failure is now never cached, also one an earlier release cached.
- **A new request in the same conversation is its own requirement.** A request declared after the session's evidence was finalized reads as `intent/<session>-c2` (Shale's continuation file), so it is checked against the first one instead of being missed.
- The pre-commit hook no longer asks to run `stele adopt` when there is nothing to adopt, such as a Shale project before its first request.
- Approving a Shale request no longer fails with "changed since the check": Shale records each command into the open session, so the check now fingerprints the declared requests rather than Shale's files.
- Retiring a request that was approved earlier on the same branch now removes its rules in the same change; before, the conflict was asked again.
- A second change on a branch whose first change is approved is named `<branch>-2`, so it no longer writes over the approved change's registry files, answers and card.
- When the base branch predates the approved registry (Stele was set up on this branch), the refusal names the commit that added the registry, to compare with until it reaches the base.
- A PR card records a conflict decision without the solver's example situation, which is for the agent explaining it.
- The first change's approval question no longer says it agrees with an approved spec when nothing is approved yet.
- The change report tells the owner to approve by answering the approval question, as the skill does, instead of with `stele approve`.
- `stele change shale --help` prints the help; it used to ask for `--base`.

## v0.1.0

The first public release. Stele is experimental; [what to expect](README.md#what-to-expect) states measured accuracy and cost.

- **Setup.** `stele init` detects the spec format (Spec Kit, OpenSpec, Kiro or plain Markdown) and the coding agents in the repository, and writes Stele's instructions, a skill where the agent has skills, the agent hooks and a git pre-commit hook. Agents: Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Windsurf, VS Code (Copilot), Gemini CLI and opencode.
- **Adoption.** `stele adopt` translates every requirement once into rules over one vocabulary, checks the rules against each other with Z3, and asks the owner about each contradiction in the spec's own words.
- **Changes.** `stele change openspec|speckit|kiro` checks one spec change against every approved requirement, translating only what the change adds or edits. `stele change answer` records the owner's answers; approvals are pinned to the requirement's text.
- **Prevention.** Before the agent plans, the prompt hook adds the approved requirements a request touches (`stele rules`). After an edit, the hook reports saved specs that no longer match their approved rules. Neither hook calls a model.
- **Gate.** `stele gate` in CI and `stele precommit` locally block unapproved or conflicting rules.
- **Rules.** Permissions, preconditions and invariants over states and steps, with step results, multiple instances, durations and `min`, `max`, `abs`. Each conflict comes with a minimal set of requirements and a counterexample.
- **Cost control.** `--max-cost` caps model spend for `stele adopt` and `stele change`; a stopped run keeps the work it finished.
- **Install.** `install.sh` and Homebrew (`provasign/tap/stele`), for Linux and macOS on amd64 and arm64. Z3 is required on PATH.
- **Shale requests as specs.** `stele change shale . --base main` checks the requests a branch's agents declared with `shale intent` against every approved one, including a session still open, before the agent edits. `stele init` picks this format in a repository with `.shale/` and no spec folder. A conflict with an earlier request offers to retire it (`.stele/retired-intents.yaml`) or to restate both as one request.
- **Code touches.** `stele touches` lists the approved requirements the branch's code touches, from Grove's parse of the changed files: a function named like a rule's step, or code that is, reads or writes an attribute the rule reads. The post-edit hook says so when that set changes.
- **PR card.** `stele change`, the owner's answers and the pre-commit hook keep `.stele/cards/<change>.json` current: status, requirements added, changed and retired, the owner's decisions, and the approved requirements the code touches. Shale renders it on the PR.
- The watch hook's message names the command that checks pending statements.
